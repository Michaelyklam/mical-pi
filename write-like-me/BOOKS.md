# Book notes / reactions

Source: `Documents/Michael's Thoughts/Books` (~19 files).

## Structure
- Freeform, one file per book (occasionally one per chapter, e.g. "Chapter 2 Luck and Risk.md"). Length ranges from one-line stubs to 2,000+ words.
- Longer notes use loose headers matching the book's own chapter/section titles — no fixed template.
- No consistent takeaways section, but several end with an explicit "Final thoughts" or "Reflection" paragraph.
- A numeric "Overall Rating: X/10" appears at the top on some, not most — optional, don't force it.
- Nearly every file opens with a hashtag line: `#Reading` plus a topic tag (`#Christianity`, `#Finance`, `#Communication`, `#Reflection`).
- Heavy wikilinks — to other book notes and to concept stub-notes he's building out (`[[enough]]`, `[[Trust]]`), plus block-embeds pulling exact passages.

## Sentence & paragraph style
- Summary and reaction are interwoven, not separated: state the author's point in 1–2 sentences, then immediately pivot to his own take — usually with an explicit pivot word: "But my perspective on this is...", "I feel differently...", "I wonder if..."
- Informal, run-on sentences favoring commas over periods; comma splices are frequent.
- Constant, direct first-person commentary ("I feel like...", "I think...").
- Poses open rhetorical questions to himself mid-note and often doesn't resolve them.

## Formatting habits
- Mostly prose paragraphs. Bullets/numbered lists only for a book's own discrete lesson-list.
- Minimal bold/italic — italics occasionally for a book's key term.
- Headers borrow the book's own chapter/section names, not a generic template.
- Quotes from secular books are paraphrased inline rather than blockquoted; Bible references (when relevant) use the `> [!bible]` callout.

## Tone & vocabulary
- Casual-reflective, first-person, conversational even on analytical topics (finance, psychology) — not academic.
- Generally receptive to secular self-help ideas but explicitly reframes them through a Christian lens, and openly disagrees when a premise clashes with his worldview ("a very disagreeable one," "I feel differently").
- Recurring phrasing: "I feel like," "I think," "I wonder if," "makes sense," "definitely."

## Verbatim calibration
- "This voice is an evolutionary superpower that we have... But this inner voice is a double edged sword." (Chatter - Ethan Kross)
- "As a christian, the definition of enough comes from God. That's why we can earn little be happy and still have more to pour out into others." (Chapter 3 Never Enough)
- "I feel like I can really see the value in each of the things people have designed... But we live in a fallen world, these things would always have a chance to be corrupted." (If Cats Disappeared From the World)
