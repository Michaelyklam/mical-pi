# Bible study / sermon notes / faith reflection

Source: `Documents/Michael's Thoughts/Faith` (`Bible/`, `Sermons/`, `Ministry/`, loose files like Lent reflections).

## Structure
- Short-to-medium: independent studies 100–500 words, sermon notes 300–600, some teaching/chapter notes 2,000+.
- Older/independent-study voice: unstructured running prose or loose bullets, with verse numbers used inline as informal section markers ("4:12-21").
- Newer (2025–2026) entries use real markdown headers, a `**Passage:**`/`**Date:**` metadata block, and `---` rules between sections.
- Sermon notes almost always open with a `> [!bible]` callout quoting the anchor passage, then mix running commentary with verse-by-verse breakdown.
- Tags are usually inline hashtags at the top (`#BibleStudy`, `#Sermon`, `#Christianity`) rather than YAML frontmatter. Wikilinks used liberally for people, books, concepts.
- No signature close — trails off after the last reflective sentence, sometimes under an explicit "Application" section.

## Sentence & paragraph style
- Older/independent-study voice: long, comma-spliced, run-on — thoughts stack with "and," "but," "so."
- Explanation proceeds inductively: restate the passage → ask a direct question ("Why does God want to kill Moses?") → work toward an answer/application. Rhetorical questions are a core device, sometimes stacked two or three in a row.
- First-person application is usually its own final section, kept separate from third-person exposition of the text — don't blend "here's what the text means" and "here's what it means for me" sentence-by-sentence.
- Newer entries (Lent reflections, Devos) are tighter, shorter, with colon-led setups: "What this made me think:"

## Formatting habits
- Bullets for lists of facts/promises/observations; prose for reflection and application.
- Bold sparing — section labels only (`**Passage:**`), not mid-sentence emphasis.
- Blockquotes reserved almost exclusively for scripture, often the `[!bible]` callout with a linked reference title.
- No checkboxes in this genre.

## Tone & vocabulary
- Reflective and conversational, leaning teacherly when unpacking a passage (naming original-language nuance, citing commentary), then shifting personal and vulnerable in application.
- Recurring phrases: "What am I getting out of this?", "Takeaway:", "Application wise", "I'm not sure if..."
- Recurring vocabulary: covenant, sovereignty, identity/foundation, testimony, repent/repentance, glory, faithful/faithfulness, kingdom of God.
- Signature framing device: name a "lie"/false belief, then counter it with a "truth" grounded in identity-in-Christ language (performance, people-pleasing, control, victim).

## Verbatim calibration
- "Work done in his name, but without him is nothing." (Exodus 4 - Independent study)
- "I take PRIDE in my performance and abilities. and that is my false foundation." (Soul Care - Chapter 1 Identity)
- "So I keep asking myself: is this prayer asking for fruit for my own use, or is it asking for what I can steward for His glory?" (Devo, 1 Samuel 2 — Hannah)

## Checklist
- [ ] Exposition (what the text means) and application (what it means for him) kept as distinct sections, not interwoven
- [ ] At least one genuine rhetorical question driving the reasoning
- [ ] Scripture quoted via blockquote, not paraphrased inline
- [ ] Ends on the application/reflection, no formal wrap-up
- [ ] "Lie → truth" framing when the note is about identity/performance/self-worth
