# Self-improvement / communication frameworks

Source: `Documents/Michael's Thoughts/Personal` (~100 files — things like "3 Tips to Always have something to say," "Four Stages of Competency," "Some thoughts on charisma").

## Structure
- Mostly short: 1–15 lines. A handful of developed ones (storytelling, charisma, eye contact) run long with multiple `###` headers.
- Opens with a bare tag line, no YAML frontmatter: `#framework #Communication #Charisma`, `#Concept`.
- Tags are freeform combinations, not a controlled vocabulary.
- Heavy mid-sentence wikilinks to other frameworks, sometimes with pipe aliases (`[[80/20 Rule|80/20]]`).
- Layout is freeform prose broken by blank lines rather than strict outlines, though numbered/named steps appear when the framework itself has discrete stages (Four Stages, SCQA).

## Sentence & paragraph style
- Short, punchy notes-to-self mixed with slightly longer explanatory run-ons — rarely more than two clauses joined by a comma or "because."
- Frequent imperative/coaching voice: "Be careful speaking about work," "Ask questions that are likely to bring out opinions."
- First-person asides are rare here — he mostly writes in second-person imperative or general statements ("Everyone likes to talk about themselves").

## Formatting habits
- Bold/italic barely used at all.
- Headers only in the longer, more developed notes.
- Bulleted lists (`-`) are the default for examples/sub-points; numbered lists specifically for sequential/ranked frameworks.
- Example scripted dialogue is given as inline quoted sentences, not blockquotes: `"Can I ask you a random question?"`

## Tone & vocabulary
- Pragmatic, self-coaching, drill-sergeant-to-self — treats social skills as trainable "frameworks" and "reps."
- Recurring words: "framework," "condense," "distill," "signal," "genuine," "deepen the conversation."
- Signature move: reducing advice to punchy parallel-structure lines — "Steer don't follow," "Echo and Expand," "Ask to learn, not just to connect."

## Verbatim calibration
- "Be careful speaking about work." (3 Tips to Always have something to say)
- "Charisma is being able to let someone else feel like the main character." (Some thoughts on charisma)
- "Don't tell people things that happened, show them things that happened." (How To Tell A Story - The Moth)

## Checklist
- [ ] At least one punchy parallel-structure line summarizing the core idea ("X, not Y")
- [ ] Imperative/second-person coaching voice, not first-person narration
- [ ] Concrete scripted example lines in quotes where relevant, not abstract description
- [ ] Numbered steps only if the framework genuinely has sequential stages — otherwise loose prose + bullets
