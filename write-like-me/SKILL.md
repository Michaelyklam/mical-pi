# Write Like Me

## Pick the register first

**Reflective** (journal, faith/Bible study, book notes, entertainment reactions) — first person, hedged, thinking out loud:
- Hedge constantly: "I think," "maybe," "to be seen," "I wonder if" — commit to a take while leaving room to revise it. Don't flatten into false confidence.
- Calibrate by comparison, not absolute judgment: against other days, other books, other shows/scores.
- Use genuine rhetorical questions to drive the reasoning — it's fine to leave one unresolved.
- When a piece does conclude something, distill it into an itemized form — a lessons-learned list, a "lie → truth" pairing — rather than staying in flowing prose.
- Trail off at the end. No "in conclusion," no wrap-up restatement.

**Instructional** (recipes, self-improvement/communication frameworks, work/career notes) — imperative/second-person, much less hedging:
- Tell the reader what to do, not what you're thinking about doing.
- Distill to enumerated or punchy parallel-structure lines ("Steer, don't follow"), a numbered recap after a process, or a tip called out as its own line — not paragraphs of reasoning.
- Stay conversational and often unpolished (comma splices, fragments) — this is not corporate-clean writing, just less hedged.

**Always, in either register:**
- Bold is only ever a structural label (`**Tip:**`, `**Passage:**`, `**Scope:**`) — never decorative emphasis.
- Cross-reference people/places/other notes liberally (wikilink-style, or natural inline mentions outside a note-taking tool).
- Don't manufacture typos or broken grammar to seem authentic. Match the looseness of construction (run-ons, fragments) without faking misspellings — and don't over-polish into brand-copy smoothness either.

## Structure by genre

- **Journal:** open with a topic tag, then prose. No fixed template beyond that. Length should swing with how eventful the thing being described is — a quiet day gets one line, don't pad it.
- **Faith/Bible study:** quote scripture as a blockquote. Keep exposition (what the text means) and application (what it means personally) as separate sections — never blend them sentence-by-sentence.
- **Books:** state the author's point in 1-2 sentences, then pivot explicitly ("But my take is...") into a personal reaction. Reframe through a faith lens when it genuinely fits — don't force it.
- **Entertainment:** give a numeric rating with an actual one-line rationale for the specific number. Calibrate mainly by comparing to other titles.
- **Recipes:** fixed order — About → Ingredients (checkboxes) → numbered Instructions → Tips/Storage → tags. Steps are one-clause imperatives; any warning or reasoning goes in its own `**Tip:**` sub-bullet, never inline in the step. Metric units, scalable quantities.
- **Personal frameworks:** second-person imperative, boiled down to a punchy parallel-structure line. Use numbered steps only if the framework genuinely has sequential stages.
- **Work/career:** headers mirror the actual prompt or process being answered. Self-evals pair every acknowledged gap with a concrete next step. Close a process-heavy section with a short numbered recap.

Don't force this voice onto CRM-style contact notes, trip/logistics tables, technical specs, or meeting-note fragments — those aren't prose genres. Flag it if a request needs a mode not listed above rather than forcing a fit.

## Before handing back a draft
- [ ] Right register (hedged/reflective vs imperative/instructional) — not a blend of both
- [ ] Bold used only as a label, if at all
- [ ] Ends on the actual last thought/step — no forced wrap-up sentence (reflective) or unnecessary restatement (instructional)
- [ ] Doesn't read like it could've been copy-edited by a brand style guide
